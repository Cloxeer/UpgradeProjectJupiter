THE PROCESSESUpgrade

Five things happen on this campus that their site does not explain. Each one below is a real machine with real limits. The sliders show the limits too. A plan that leaves them out is a plan that gets thrown out.

Words used on this page · tap to open
MW
One megawatt is a million watts, a rate of energy flow. As heat, 1 MW is about 38 home gas furnaces running flat out. As electricity, 1 MW is roughly what 750 U.S. homes draw on average. The campus needs 2,462 MW of electricity and throws off about the same in heat.[44][45][4]
MGD
Million gallons per day, the standard unit for water plants. 1 MGD is about the daily use of 3,300 homes at 300 gallons a home.[46]
tons/yr
Tons per year is how air permits count pollution. A ton is 2,000 pounds; a full dump truck carries about 10 tons.
ppb
Parts per billion. The health standard for ozone (smog) is 70 ppb averaged over 8 hours; above that, EPA says lungs are being harmed.[47]
µg/m³
Micrograms per cubic meter, the unit for fine particles (PM2.5). The health standard is 9 µg/m³ averaged over a year.[48]
CO₂e
Carbon-dioxide equivalent. Methane and other gases are converted to the amount of CO₂ that would warm the planet the same amount, so one number covers them all.
capture efficiency
Not the same as purity. Purity (about 95%) is how much of the exhaust is CO₂. Efficiency (90–95%) is how much of that CO₂ the unit removes. Whatever it misses is released.[3][15]
brackish
Water with 1,000 to 10,000 milligrams of dissolved salt per liter. Seawater is about 35,000. It looks clear; the salt is dissolved.[19]
PSD
A federal permitting review that applies when a plant emits 250 tons a year or more of a pollutant. It requires the best available controls. The fuel-cell permit's pollutants are far below that line (NOx 37 tons a year), so the review does not apply; the ask on this site is continuous monitoring instead.[3]
Title V
Title V of the Clean Air Act: the operating permit every major source must hold, with monitoring and public reporting.[3]
nonattainment
EPA's label for an area whose air fails a health standard. Sunland Park has been an ozone nonattainment area since Aug. 3, 2018.[49]
acre-foot
An acre-foot is 325,851 gallons, about what three homes use in a year.
IRB
Industrial revenue bonds. The county holds title to the project and leases it back, so the developer pays negotiated payments instead of property tax for 30 years. The lease is the county's enforcement tool.[1][12]
CBA
The Community Benefits Agreement signed Nov. 12, 2025. It sets the binding jobs (750 full-time), the water cap, the $11.4M in programs and the site size (about 819 acres).[1]

Process 1 · Heat

Where the heat goes

Net gain for humanity ·free heat that was going to the sky now warms greenhouse roots in winter and preheats the water plant all year; summer cooling stays evaporative.

How can heat make cooling? And is there a winter here? ▾

Can heat make cooling? An absorption chiller runs on hot water instead of electricity, the way a propane camping fridge does, but standard units want water near 90 °C and this loop is 45 to 65 °C, so no summer cooling is counted here. Winter is real here: Santa Teresa nights drop into the 20s and 30s °F from December to February, so a greenhouse needs root heat on every cold night from November to March. This is not about making more power: the heat is already there and free. Their plan blows it into the sky; ours puts it to work so 150 acres of greenhouses grow food year-round. The gain is measured in food and jobs, not megawatts.

Zero is not on the slider on purpose. Capture designs top out around 90 to 95%; the longest-running power-plant unit, Boundary Dam, captured 848,388 tonnes in 2024 at 85% availability against a 1-million-tonne design, so the slider starts at 90, not 95[64][65]. Use before storage: curing concrete with CO₂ and making carbonate aggregate are commercial (690,000 tonnes mineralized worldwide to date; one aggregate plant takes 5,000 tonnes a year)[95][96], so the use slider starts at 1% and grows only as buyers sign; the pipeline to storage is where most of it goes, Process 4 shrinks the whole stream as the gas share falls, and the federal 45Q credit pays $85 a ton stored or used[89]. The rest is closed over time by blending renewable gas and hydrogen, which Bloom hardware runs on[51], plus verified removals; HB93's 2045 net-zero definition allows methane offsets, so the physical route is a choice[1][78]. Storage depth: CO₂ stays a dense fluid below about 800 m / 2,625 ft under impermeable cap rock[52]. New Mexico has no permitted Class VI wells yet, so the realistic path is a pipeline to Permian Basin storage[53]. That line crosses the same kind of land the gas pipeline has been denied on twice, and Texas Class VI reviews run about a year, so the plan installs the skids with the fuel cells but counts metered capture only from year 5[10][53].

Also feasible here, not the plan: robotic vertical farms (Japan, China), with the numbers

Two working examples. Techno Farm Keihanna (Spread Co., Kyoto) grows about 30,000 heads of lettuce a day in stacked trays; robots seed, move and harvest, an IoT system tunes light, temperature and nutrients, the farm runs at a 99% operating rate and recycles over 90% of its water[25]. Chengdu (Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, 2023): a 20-layer, 8.8-metre plant factory on a 100-square-metre footprint where robot arms do everything from seeding to packing; lettuce in 35 days, about 50 tonnes a year, the output of roughly 4 hectares of field[26].

The catch is electricity.Today's vertical farms use about 10–18 kWh per kilogram of lettuce against about 3.8 for a Dutch greenhouse; the best measured cases reach 3–7[27]. LEDs cannot use the site's waste heat, so on this campus every vertical-farm kilowatt is a gas kilowatt until Process 4 retires it. That is why the plan is glass greenhouses on free heat first, with vertical farms as a phase-two option once clean power is on the wire[28].

THE RECEIPTSUpgrade

Fuel-cell exhaust is concentrated enough to capture cheaply

Bloom Energy's own materials: about ten times the CO₂ concentration of a gas turbine, in one fifteenth the gas volume. Bloom and Chart Industries announced a carbon-capture partnership for exactly this stream.

Bloom / Chart announcement ↗
The emissions numbers are theirs, not ours

Draft Permit 10883 (Yucca Growth Infrastructure), Part A, Table 102.A: 8,820,970 tons of CO₂e a year, NOx 37.2, CO 161.2, VOC 124.0, PM 75.4; the Statement of Basis removed the applicant's 15% safety factor that had produced 10,144,115.

NMED permit 10883 ↗
The water plant is already designed and priced

Dr. Pei Xu, NMSU Civil Engineering, to the Legislature's Science, Technology and Telecommunications Committee, Oct. 31, 2023: 5 MGD brackish RO for Santa Teresa, 75% recovery, $115.5M plant, $269.5M system, brine to deep injection wells. Mesilla Basin holds ~65 million acre-feet of recoverable water.

NMSU study (PDF) ↗
Brackish desalination works in this exact desert

El Paso's Kay Bailey Hutchison plant has treated Hueco Bolson brackish water since 2007 at 27.5 MGD, piping brine 22 miles to three injection wells. It is 30 miles from Santa Teresa.

El Paso Water ↗
The layout is theirs

Every building in our plan is in the position shown on Project Jupiter Together's labeled site render dated Aug. 27, 2026. We add nothing inside their fence.

Data center heat already grows food elsewhere

Gothenburg, Sweden runs a greenhouse on data center heat with the city energy company. Dutch policy pushes data centers to supply heat networks. Equinix's Paris PA10 grows produce on the roof.

Sweden.se ↗
They signed for 750 jobs, not 1,500

The agreement between the developers and Doña Ana County requires 2,500 construction workers and 750 full-time workers once operational. The 7,000 and 1,500 figures on their website are marketing projections, not obligations.

El Paso Matters ↗
The Supreme Court just refused to unfreeze the permit

On Sept. 1, 2026 the New Mexico Supreme Court unanimously denied the developers' bid to partially reopen the air-permit proceeding. The Sept. 14 hearing on Permit 10883 will not happen while the stay stands.

Albuquerque Journal ↗
The permit really is frozen

The New Mexico Supreme Court stayed the air-permit proceeding and an emergency well authorization in August 2026. The State Land Office has denied the pipeline route twice.
