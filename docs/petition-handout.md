# Force Upgrade Project Jupiter — petition handout

Paste this into the Google Form description, or print it for the commission meeting.

## What this petition asks

We do not ask Doña Ana County to cancel Project Jupiter. We ask the county to negotiate six conditions into the $165 billion bond lease at its next consent point, before the first fuel cell turns on. Same buildings, same fence, same schedule, about 8% of the $50 billion first phase (2.5% of the $165 billion bond cap, a ceiling, not cash), most of it repayable by the federal capture credit. Each condition below says what we ask, what the company's filed plan does today, and why it matters.

**1. Catch the CO₂ at the stack before power-on.**
We ask: capture-ready fuel-cell stacks from the first day of operation, with capture metered against a 90% target once the storage line is connected (about year 5) and the results public.
Their plan today: 8.8 million tons of CO₂ a year permitted (10.1 million applied for) go straight into the sky, more than Albuquerque and Las Cruces combined. No capture is planned.
Why it matters: the exhaust is about 95% CO₂ once dried, the easiest kind to catch; the maker of the fuel cells has announced the equipment, no plant runs it yet, and the federal 45Q credit pays $85 a ton toward it.

**2. Every stack on a public meter, with limits set for the capture configuration.**
We ask: a continuous public air monitor on every fuel-cell cluster, with permit limits set for the capture configuration.
Their plan today: the draft permit estimates 37 tons of NOx a year from four tests of one 65 kW unit, scaled to 2,275 stacks, and requires no continuous stack monitor.
Why it matters: Sunland Park has failed the federal ozone standard since 2018, and this would be the largest fuel-cell plant in the world by about a hundred times. Public meters let anyone check the air the same day.

**3. Offer the computers' heat to greenhouses next door.**
We ask: the computers' waste heat offered to greenhouse growers on the empty acres beside the cooling fans, local growers first.
Their plan today: all the heat, about 2,462 megawatts, is blown into the desert by fans. The land beside them stays bare.
Why it matters: about 150 acres of greenhouses would grow up to about 60 million pounds of food a year and add 600 to 1,000 jobs. Dutch data centers already sit beside greenhouse growers, and Germany requires data centers to reuse a share of their heat.

**4. Fund the NMSU water plant, hand it to the towns' utility, recharge the towns' reclaimed water, and leave the unused farm water right in the aquifer.**
We ask: the NMSU-designed plant built and handed to the towns' utility, making 5 million gallons a day of clean water from salty groundwater; the towns' reclaimed water put back into the fresh aquifer under a state storage-and-recovery permit; and the unused part of the old farm's water right left in the aquifer in a State Engineer conservation program instead of pumped.
Their plan today: drinking water is taken from the local utility and more is pumped from the fresh aquifer. The signed deal has the companies fund a $250,000 study; the county is now designing a smaller plant with its own Jupiter tax money.
Why it matters: the plant is already designed and priced at $269.5 million, about 0.54% of the first phase. With the towns' fresh wells pumping far less and about 2 million gallons a day of reclaimed water going back underground, as El Paso has done since 1985 and Rio Rancho since 2017, the local decline slows.

**5. Tie the tax break to real jobs, with a training school on site.**
We ask: the county tax break paid only as permanent jobs are verified each year, and a $50 million training institute on site so the jobs go to people who live here.
Their plan today: the signed agreement binds the company to 750 jobs and $4 million for workforce programs. Whether missing the 750 is a lease default is not public; the leases are not posted.
Why it matters: with greenhouses, the water plant and capture, about 3,000 permanent jobs are possible. The county's own IRB policy already allows repayment or higher payments when job goals are missed; verification makes the number real instead of advertised.

**6. A closure and monitoring bond, so the land and wells are cared for after the lease ends.**
We ask: a closure and monitoring bond posted in the lease, sized by an independent engineer and revised every five years, so the wells, the land and the public meters are cared for after the companies are gone.
Their plan today: the signed agreement's obligations run only as long as the bonds are outstanding. It has no decommissioning, restoration or bond clause; after the 30-year lease the shareholders own the site.
Why it matters: servers last five years, the lease thirty, the aquifer and the stored CO₂ far longer. Doña Ana County already requires this bond of solar farms. It is the only instrument written to outlive the companies.

## After you sign

This petition is a count of support, not a legal instrument. We hand the names and ZIP codes to the Doña Ana County commissioners at a public meeting before the next lease vote and to the legislators sponsoring the bills. What binds is the record: a written comment on NMED Permit 10883 and three minutes at the microphone on a second or fourth Tuesday. Then spread the word: tell your friends and family, call your commissioner, learn, understand and teach others.
