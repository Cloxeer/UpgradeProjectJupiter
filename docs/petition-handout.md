# Force Upgrade Project Jupiter — petition handout

Paste this into the Google Form description, or print it for the commission meeting.

## What this petition asks

We do not ask Doña Ana County to cancel Project Jupiter. We ask the county to write five conditions into the $165 billion bond lease before the first fuel cell turns on. Same buildings, same fence, same schedule, about 1.5% more cost. Each condition below says what we ask, what the company's filed plan does today, and why it matters.

**1. Catch the carbon from day one.**
We ask: carbon capture installed on the fuel-cell stacks from the first day of operation, catching at least 90% of the CO₂.
Their plan today: about 10 million tons of CO₂ a year go straight into the sky, more than Albuquerque and Las Cruces combined. No capture is planned.
Why it matters: the exhaust is about 95% CO₂ once dried, the easiest kind to catch, and the maker of the fuel cells already sells the equipment.

**2. One air permit, and a public air meter on every stack.**
We ask: one permit for the whole campus that accepts the strict federal review, with a public air monitor on every stack.
Their plan today: the filing keeps each smog gas just under 250 tons a year, the amount that would trigger the strict review and the best pollution controls.
Why it matters: Sunland Park has failed the federal ozone standard since 2018. Public meters let anyone check the air the same day.

**3. Use the heat to grow food.**
We ask: the computers' waste heat offered to greenhouse growers on the empty acres beside the cooling fans, local growers first.
Their plan today: all the heat, about 2,400 megawatts, is blown into the desert by fans. The land beside them stays bare.
Why it matters: about 150 acres of greenhouses would grow about 60 million pounds of food a year and add about 1,000 jobs. Gothenburg, Sweden does this today on data-center heat.

**4. Build the water plant that is already designed.**
We ask: the NMSU-designed plant built and handed to the local utility, making 5 million gallons a day of clean water from salty groundwater.
Their plan today: drinking water is taken from the local utility and more is pumped from the fresh aquifer. The signed deal funds only a $250,000 study of the plant.
Why it matters: the plant is designed and priced at $269 million, about 0.16% of the bond. It gives back more water than the campus takes.

**5. Real jobs, verified every year, for people who live here.**
We ask: the county tax break paid only as permanent jobs are verified each year, and a $50 million training institute on site.
Their plan today: the signed agreement binds the company to 750 jobs and $4 million for workforce programs, with the tax break paid regardless.
Why it matters: with greenhouses, the water plant and capture, about 3,000 permanent jobs are possible. Verification makes the number real instead of advertised.
