# The Jupiter Fact Test — audit of every claim on the site (Sept. 6, 2026)

> **Status, Sept. 7, 2026:** every P0 and P1 correction below was applied to the site text (no UI change) and deployed. Remaining P2 hygiene items were folded in where found. The 250-year view keeps its estimate framing per the site owner, with every figure labelled an estimate and our CO₂ residual now accruing for as long as gas runs.

Method: six parallel reviews (heat & food; carbon & air; water; power; money & law; logic & long views), each putting every claim through four trials: (T1) does the cited document say it, (T2) does the arithmetic hold, (T3) has it been done at scale, (T4) is it a net gain over now, 30 and 250 years once its cons are counted. The two most consequential findings were then re-checked against the primary documents by hand (NMED draft Statement of Basis and draft Permit Part A for NSR 10883, both saved in `docs/research/primary/`).

## Verdict

The core idea survives: keep the campus, capture the CO₂, reuse the heat, make water from the brackish layer, meter everything, bond the closure, tie the tax break to jobs. Each of those has a real precedent and a real lever.

But the site as it stands cannot be shown to a permit writer, a bond lawyer or a utility engineer without being discredited in one slide, because three load-bearing claims are wrong, one plan element added today contradicts our own numbers, and about a dozen supporting numbers are unsourced or stale. Roughly: of ~90 claims audited, about 45 are verified, about 20 are labelled estimates that hold, about 15 are wrong or backwards, and about 10 are stale (they describe the withdrawn turbine plan) or unsourced.

## The three load-bearing errors

### 1. The permit numbers are backwards (carbon & air; verified by hand)

Draft Permit 10883, Part A, Table 102.A, facility-wide potential to emit:

| Pollutant | tons/yr |
|---|---|
| NOx | 37.2 |
| CO | 161.21 |
| VOC | 124.0 |
| SO₂ | 0.08 |
| PM / PM10 / PM2.5 | 75.4 |
| HAPs | 1.4 |
| GHG as CO₂e | 8,820,970 |

Statement of Basis §16.D: "A 15% safety factor has been removed from the applicant's GHG emission calculations… AQB will not accept/allow the addition of a safety factor in the calculation to increase the GHG emissions by 1,323,145 tpy, from 8,820,970 tpy to 10,144,115 tpy."

So: the permitted figure is **8.82 million tons**; 10,144,115 is the applicant's number that NMED struck. The site says the reverse. No pollutant is "just under 250"; NOx is 37. PSD does not apply because the source is not in a listed category and each pollutant is under 250, which is the ordinary rule for any new plant, not a loophole. It is one NSR permit and one Title V major source (Title V because CO and VOC exceed 100 tpy). The "split permits / NOx ~500 tpy / 13–14 Mt" figures come from the withdrawn East/West gas-turbine applications. The facility is outside the Sunland Park nonattainment area (SoB §2.79) and NMED modelled its ozone contribution below the screening level.

What this breaks on the site: hero, "what it is", speed table, highlights card 6, compare rows air and smog, petition demand 2, will-not list, talking points, FAQ, feasibility "permit" demand, science expert lines, blueprint power zone, one receipt. What survives and is stronger: the emissions rest on four four-hour tests of one 65 kW unit, there is no continuous stack monitoring requirement, and the facility would be the largest fuel-cell plant in the world by ~100× (NMED itself flags this in §16.E). Demand 2 should become: continuous monitors on every cluster, public data, permit limits set for the capture configuration.

Derived numbers to recompute: one binding job per ~11,800 tons (not 13,500); residual after 90–95% capture 0.44–0.88 Mt (site shows 0.5–1.0 and 0.5–1.4); 250-year as-filed total ≈2.2 Gt (not 2.5); "more than Albuquerque and Las Cruces combined" still true at 8.82 vs 6.7 Mt (Source NM, Dec. 5, 2025), and that is the right citation, not the NEE page.

### 2. "Zero gas hours from 2045, required by state law" is neither (power; statute checked)

NMSA 62-17-12(E)(2) defines a "net-zero carbon resource" as one that "emits no carbon dioxide into the atmosphere, **or that reduces methane emitted into the atmosphere in an amount equal to no less than one-tenth of the tons of carbon dioxide emitted**." A gas fuel-cell plant complies by offsetting a tenth of its CO₂ in methane cuts. HB93 requires no gas hours to fall. Separately, the site's own Blueprint calculator cannot reach zero: with every slider at maximum (500 MW geothermal, 2,000 MW PPA, all roofs) it yields about 1,258 MW average clean supply against 2,462 MW, roughly 4,470 gas-hours-equivalent a year. True zero needs ~2.7 GW of firm geothermal (3.7× The Geysers) or ~6 GW of wind and solar plus tens of GWh of storage. Google, the leader, was 66% hourly carbon-free in 2024. And SunZia's line is 3,021 MW to Pinal County, Arizona, fully contracted to California and SRP; El Paso Electric's whole system peak is below the campus load. "Delivered by wire" has no wire.

What this breaks: every long view. Our CO₂ line, the "0 gas hours since 2045" chips, the 250-year "nothing has burned here for 231 years", timeline years 20 and 250, the smog row after 2045, Process 4's framing. The honest version: HB93 lets a gas microgrid count as net-zero via methane offsets; the upgrade asks for the harder physical reading, with a falling gas-hours schedule on a public meter and a zero date decided by geothermal test wells, storage and new transmission. The "gas hours" stat should be renamed "share of the year's energy from gas".

### 3. "The county writes the six conditions into the lease" is not a power the county has (money & law)

CBA §III: amendment "only by written agreement of all the Parties." Bond Purchase Agreements §9: no amendment or modification. The bonds are held by a company affiliate, so there is no outside bondholder. A county attorney will read the site as asking the commission to repudiate a signed contract. The stronger honest version: the county has leverage points it can use to negotiate the conditions in: consent to assignment (§IV.1, "not unreasonably withheld"), discretionary support resolutions (§II), enforcement of existing defaults, and every future amendment the companies request. Sandoval County did exactly this with Intel in 2019 and 2024, adding PILT by mutual amendment when Intel needed an investment-period extension. Say "negotiate in", name the leverage, and identify the next date the companies need the county's signature; that date is the real deadline, not "before the fuel cells arrive".

## The element added today that contradicts our own numbers (water)

CRRUA demand, from NMSU: 3.1 MGD (2020), 6.0 (2027), 15.0 (2042). The plant makes 5 MGD in 2028. There is never a surplus in Phase 1, and the fresh wells do not "rest"; fresh pumping drops to ~1 MGD and climbs again unless the 10 MGD expansion happens. The recharge stream that exists is ~1.85 MGD of reclaimed water from three CRRUA plants, and today that effluent is discharged to the Rio Grande under NPDES permits and counts as the towns' return-flow offset (NMSU ll. 3795–3804); 19.25.8.14 NMAC also requires a valid water right for stored water. So "2 to 5 MGD back underground" should read "about 2 MGD of reclaimed water, once the river offset and water right are settled at the State Engineer", the 5 MGD upper bound goes, and the water gauge's climb must be labelled a schematic everywhere, not only in the expert note. The right in-state precedent is Rio Rancho Pure (2017, first NM permit to replenish groundwater with purified reclaimed water); Bear Canyon uses river water. El Paso says its recharge "slowed the decline"; stabilization came with conservation and river water.

Also: Doña Ana County voted May 23, 2025 to terminate CRRUA's joint powers agreement, and NMED sued for an independent manager. "Handed to CRRUA" names a utility that may not exist in 2028; say "the towns' utility, whatever succeeds CRRUA". And the county's STAR plan already includes 1.5 MGD of wastewater reuse, which is the county saying it wants reuse.

Money and jobs

The mineral conclusion survives; the comparison must be reworded. Brine salt load is 18,000–25,000 t/yr on the 6.7 MGD feed, not 14,000.

- "$60M heat exchanger pays back in fan and chiller electricity": fan power is a few percent of heat rejected; winter savings ≈ $1M/yr; chillers do not run on winter nights. It pays back through heat sales, if a grower signs.
- 8,000+ construction jobs, 11,000+ total, "$8B at full build", "about 5,000" (table says 4,800): no derivation. Derive or delete.
- The 1.5% uses the 30-year bond cap as denominator. Against the $50B five-year commitment the package is ~4.8% (developer ~3.9%), and the +$640M PILT (the "$1B for schools") is a developer cost missing from the cost table. Show both denominators and add the PILT line. compare.ts says $2.5B and $450M; costTotals say $2.42B and $0.47B.
- "The county is already paying $250,000 to study desalination": backwards; Exhibit B has the companies pay the county.

Say so, and note the county's own IRB Safeguards page already provides for repayment or higher PILT when job goals are missed, which makes demand 5 routine rather than novel (the site currently says it found no NM precedent).

Heat and food
- Gothenburg is a 130 m² proof-of-concept growing herbs at 24 °C, ~4,700× smaller than 150 acres; Boden is 300 m². The scale precedent is Agriport A7 (Netherlands) and the German Energy Efficiency Act's mandated 10% heat reuse from July 2026 rising to 20% in 2028. Feasibility "done at scale" should be partial.
- Absorption chillers: single-effect lithium-bromide units want ~90 °C and reject ~2.4× their cooling as heat; the site's own loop is 45–65 °C. The "twelve-month heat use" story does not close as written. Summer cooling is evaporative pads (water) or a future low-grade technology.
- 0.6 MW/acre winter peak is plausible (independent check 0.5–0.85) but is cited to the Swedish pilot, which does not say it. Annual average heat use is ~15–20 MW: about 1% of the stream, 4% on the coldest night. Say so where the site says "0 MW wasted first".
- 400,000 lb/acre is top-tier year-round tomato yield labelled "average"; a mixed crop is 30–45M lb. 6.5 jobs/acre is the high end (Marfa 4.9, Willcox 5.7, Coldwater 3).
- "Pesticide-free" is oversold (biocontrol-led, not zero; ToBRFV wrecked AppHarvest). Greenhouse evaporative cooling in Arizona measured 3–15 L/m²/day → 1–1.6 MGD in summer for 150 acres, 20–30% of the plant's output, all consumptive; not stated anywhere.
- Desal preheat: membranes cap feed near 45 °C; +15 °C may exceed it. Say +5–10 °C. The 20 MW brine concentrator on server heat needs >65–70 °C; unsupported.

Carbon
- No SOFC capture plant operates anywhere; Bloom–Chart is an announced product (Feb. 2025). "Sold today" should be partial. Best operating point-source plants sustain 50–75% (Boundary Dam's ten-year average ≈660,000 t/yr, not 850,000; Quest 75%; Gorgon 1.3 of 3.4–4 Mt design). 90–95% is a target, not a rate.
- The $1.5B capture estimate omits the extra fuel cells the energy penalty requires (250–490 MW), a ~200-mile CO₂ pipeline to the nearest Class VI storage (Stratos/Penwell, TX; ~$1B for a 24-inch line), 10–20 injection wells, storage O&M (~$80M/yr), and the 50-year post-injection bond. It also omits 45Q: $85/t for 12 years on ~7.9 Mt/yr ≈ $650–750M/yr if construction starts before 2033. Net cost may be near zero; say so with the conditions. Stratos's three wells are sized for 0.72 Mt/yr; Jupiter would need ~12×.
- The use slider starts at 10% (~0.9 Mt/yr), more than CarbonCure's entire decade worldwide; start at ≤1%.
- Energy penalty for SOFC anode-stream capture in the literature is ~4–6%; 10–20% is conservative but its cost is not in the table.
- Stack life: cite the SoB ("hot swap of power modules approximately once per five years"), not the 2010 article.

## Is the plan logical? The argument, with weak links marked

P1 The plant as filed emits 8.82 Mt CO₂/yr and the largest fuel-cell plant anywhere is ~25 MW. Sound.
P2 The binding commitments are 750 jobs, $12M/yr, a 20,000 gpd potable cap, and no closure clause. Sound.
P3 The dried anode exhaust is ~95% CO₂, so capture is unusually cheap and the vendor sells it. Half sound: purity is verified; delivered capture at 90–95% for a decade is not, anywhere.
P4 Reject heat can grow ~60M lb/yr and ~1,000 jobs on 150 acres. Sound as a labelled upper-bound estimate; unsound as "done at scale".
P5 A 5 MGD brackish plant is designed and priced; El Paso proves the method. Sound.
P6 → the fresh wells rest, surplus is recharged, the table climbs. Broken by our own demand numbers (see above); repairable as "reclaimed recharge ~2 MGD, fresh pumping falls sharply, table's decline slows, schematic".
P7 Cost ≈1.5% of the bond. Arithmetic right, denominator misleading, biggest ask omitted.
P8 Same land, same timeline. Weak: capture-ready is same-timeline; a Texas pipeline, Class VI wells (4–5 years) and an ASR permit (Bear Canyon: 2008 tests → 2014 permit) are not.
C1 Net gain for air, water, food, jobs over 30 years. Follows for air if P3 is stated as a performance condition; for food and jobs as estimates; for water once P6 is repaired.
C2 Net gain for humanity over 250 years. Asserted, not demonstrated. A "net" needs both columns. The costs the site never counts: 10–20% more gas burned for capture (and its NOx); pipeline and storage liability; brine injection; drawing a reserve whose recharge is "unknown"; 1–1.6 MGD of greenhouse cooling water; the opportunity cost of ~$2.4B plus $640M PILT; 30 years of gas lock-in that a $1.5B capture asset creates.

Circularity to remove: the $6.6B takes the developers' IMPLAN total and adds our own estimates, then the legislator voice cites "$6.6B" as if independent; the 3,000 jobs derive from our 6.5/acre × our 150 acres, then appear as "enforceable" as though a lease could conjure growers.

## Could the upgrade cause a net loss? The catastrophic-risk scan

1. Capture as a licence. A $1.5B capture asset only pays with the fuel cells running; if real-world capture is 50–75%, we will have legitimized 2–4 Mt/yr for 30 years. Mitigation (cheap): the lease condition is metered tons captured and a declining gas-hours schedule, not installed skids; 45Q requires metering anyway.
2. Energy penalty. More stacks, more NOx/CO/PM; permit limits must be set for the capture configuration, and continuous monitors must be the ask.
3. CO₂ pipeline. ~200 miles across two states; Satartia (2020) is the precedent for rupture risk. Mitigation: use first, store last; name liability in the bond.
4. Brackish pumping. Upconing of the fresh/brackish interface toward the Lanark and Noria wells could make the towns' water worse, the opposite of the promise. Mitigation: interface monitoring wells before the well field is drilled; phased pumping; El Paso measured ~0.3 m of subsidence in the Hueco.
5. Reclaimed-water recharge. NM has no indirect-potable-reuse rule; write California's groundwater replenishment standard into the lease; cite Rio Rancho Pure.
6. Greenhouses. Summer cooling water competes with homes; AppHarvest's bankruptcy is the cautionary case; 150 acres of empty glass is a real outcome. Mitigation: ring-fence the towns' 5 MGD; ask for an RFP and right of first refusal, not a completed farm.
7. Credibility. This is the largest practical risk to the net gain. The false 250-ton story, the 10.1 Mt, the "zero gas by law" and "the county writes it in" are exactly what a permit writer, a bond lawyer and a utility engineer will quote to dismiss the monitoring, bond and capture asks that are defensible. Overclaiming turns a possible net gain into a certain net loss of influence.
8. Walk-away. A ~5% surcharge on Phase 1 plus a tripled PILT may not be accepted. The site should say which asks are the price of the rest and which are tradeable; the money auditor's question: would the campaign take the precedented half now (verified-jobs PILT step-ups per county policy, closure bond per the solar code, continuous monitoring, heat right of first refusal, funding the STAR plant fully) and leave capture to the permit?

## What survives, and the least-cost proven path

Defensible now, with precedent, at low cost:
- Continuous stack monitoring on every cluster with public data (standard equipment; the permit's numbers rest on four tests of one 65 kW unit).
- A closure and monitoring bond (Doña Ana County already requires it of solar farms; NM mining and oil rules; Susquehanna County, Pa.).
- Verified-jobs PILT step-ups (the county's own IRB Safeguards policy; Rio Rancho; Las Cruces's proposed tiers; Sandoval–Intel amendments as the mechanism).
- Heat right of first refusal and an RFP to growers (Dutch plot-boundary rule; German 10–20% mandate; Agriport A7).
- Fund the county's STAR plant to the full NMSU design and its 1.5 MGD reuse component; the developer pays; reclaimed recharge under a State Engineer permit as Rio Rancho did.
- Capture: capture-ready on day one, metered capture with 45Q, a sequestration deadline, and a use-first slider that starts at 1%.
- Gas: a falling gas-hours schedule with a public meter, geothermal test wells in Phase 1, and a zero date the wells decide.

## Wise questions (from all six audits and the synthesis)

1. Which anode-exhaust configuration is in permit 10883, separated stream or air-oxidised, and does the 95%-dried figure survive the filed balance of plant?
2. If 45Q pays $85/t for 12 years on ~7.9 Mt/yr, why is the developer not proposing capture, and what does that say about deliverability?
3. What firm, non-gas resource at 2–2.5 GW is physically available to this site before 2045, and who has priced it?
4. Which reading of "net-zero carbon resource" will NMED and the PRC apply, and should the lease demand the "emits no CO₂" prong explicitly?
5. Which water right will the recharge be filed under, and does the State Engineer treat the towns' effluent as a Rio Grande offset the project cannot divert?
6. Who is the receiving utility in 2028 if CRRUA no longer exists?
7. Has anyone measured the fresh/brackish interface at Lanark and Noria over time?
8. What is the next date the companies need the county's signature? That is the deadline.
9. Has anyone obtained the Nov. 1, 2025 Lease Agreements? An IPRA request resolves the site's weakest sentence ("the tax break paid regardless").
10. If growers, CO₂ buyers and geothermal developers never arrive, what does the lease deliver on its own, and is that still a net gain?
11. Is "$1B for schools" a demand you would trade, or the price of the rest?
12. What is the net CO₂ per kWh delivered over the plant's life including capture energy, pipeline, compression and injection, and at what leakage rate does capture stop being a gain?

## Recommended framing for the 250-year view

Keep the picture, change what it counts. Flows stop at the last documented horizon (the 30-year lease; 2045 for gas). Stocks persist: CO₂ already emitted (theirs ~170–280 Mt through 2045–2056 at the permitted rate; ours ~9–17 Mt), CO₂ as carbonate or under cap rock, water drawn versus recharged to date, and who is named to watch (nobody vs. the bond). Everything after year 30 is one line: "if renewed, repeat." Every number then traces to a filing, and neither column is a strawman.
